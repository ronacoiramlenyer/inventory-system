Quick Guide

For Lab Custodians

Everything you need for your everyday work in the laboratory, explained simply.

1. Logging In

  1. Open the Lab Management System website.
  2. Type in the username and password given to you by your admin.
  3. You'll land on your Dashboard — a summary of your laboratory.

2. Opening Your Laboratory

  1. Click Laboratories on the left menu.
  2. Click your laboratory's name from the list.
  3. You'll see a row of tabs across the top — these are all the forms for that laboratory (Inventory, Equipment, Schedules, Requests, and more). Click any tab to open that form.

3. Counting Your Inventory

This is the Inventory Sheet — where you record how much of each supply you actually have.

  1. Open your laboratory, then click the Inventory Sheet tab.
  2. For each item, type in the quantity you actually counted.
  3. Every item needs a category. Tick the rows that still say "No category", pick one from the dropdown at the top, and click Assign. The tick box in the header row selects every row at once. The three categories are Equipment, Tools & Materials and Consumables, and you can re-tag an item later the same way.
  4. Click Save if you just want to keep your count as a draft for now.
  5. Click Apply Adjustments to Stock when you're sure the count is final — this updates the official stock balance so it matches what you counted.
New item you don't see on the list? You can add it directly on this page, then count it the same way.
This sheet counts quantities, not serial numbers. A row here says "3D Printer — 5", not which five machines. The moment you tag a row as Equipment and save, the system opens one 201 file per unit on F-LAB-001, and that is where each machine's own serial number goes. See Recording Equipment next.

4. Recording Equipment

This is the Equipment Monitoring Record — the 201 file for each machine in your lab. Every physical unit gets its own, so five identical 3D printers are five records, not one.

Equipment is not added here — it comes from the Inventory Sheet. Tag an item as Equipment there and save; the system immediately opens one 201 file per unit in that item's balance. Count five, get five. Raise the count to seven later and two more appear on their own.

To record a unit's serial number and location:

  1. Open your laboratory, then click the Equipment Record tab. You'll see every unit listed separately — "3D Printer · unit 1", "· unit 2", and so on. A banner at the top tells you how many still need a serial number, and each of those shows Not set.
  2. Click the unit's name to open its own 201 file.
  3. Fill in Equipment ID / Serial Number and Location, then Save. You're taken back to the equipment list, so you can work straight down it.
Why it matters: the Maintenance Schedule, Calibration Schedule and Work Requests all pick a unit from this list and copy its serial number and location across. Filling these in once here means you never re-type them, and they can't disagree between forms.

To log a service entry by hand:

  1. Open the unit's record and click + Add Entry.
  2. Fill in the date, what service was performed (Preventive, Repair, or Calibration), the related Work Request ID (if any), and the status.
  3. Click Save Entry.
Most entries write themselves. When a Maintenance, Calibration or Work Request is marked Completed, the system logs it into that unit's 201 file for you. Add entries by hand only for work that never went through a request.

When a machine leaves the lab — sold, condemned, transferred — click Retire on its row. Its 201 file and full service history are kept for audit, the quantity on the Inventory Sheet drops by one, and no replacement unit is generated.

5. Maintenance & Calibration Schedules

  1. Open your laboratory, then click the Schedules tab (Preventive Maintenance or Equipment Calibration).
  2. Click + Add Row.
  3. Choose the unit from the dropdown — it lists each machine separately with its serial number, and picking one fills in the serial number and location from that unit's 201 file. Those two boxes are read-only here on purpose: F-LAB-001 is where they're corrected.
  4. Pick a frequency. Anything other than Annual generates a full year of dated rows in one go — Monthly makes twelve, spaced a month apart from the date you enter.
  5. Fill in the scheduled date, then Save. Once the work is actually done, come back and fill in the actual date.

6. Requesting an Equipment Repair

  1. Open your laboratory, then click the Work Requests tab.
  2. Click + New Request, pick the unit that needs work — same per-machine list as the Equipment Record, with the serial number and location filled in for you — and describe the problem.
  3. Submit it. Your Subject Coordinator will review and approve it, then it goes to the office to get worked on.
  4. You can check back on this same page to see its status (Pending, Filed, In Progress, Completed).
  5. When it's marked Completed, the repair is written into that unit's 201 file automatically — you don't have to log it again on F-LAB-001.

7. Borrowing Something

The left-menu Borrowing Requests page only lists requests from every lab — to file a new one, start from your own laboratory.

  1. Open your laboratory, then click the Borrowing Requests tab.
  2. Click + New Request and fill in the borrower's name, department, date needed, return date, and purpose.
  3. Click + Add Item for each item being borrowed, then submit.
  4. Your Subject Coordinator approves it directly, and it's ready to print.
  5. Print it, have the borrower sign it by hand, then scroll down to Signed Hardcopy and click + Attach Signed Copy to upload a photo of the signed printout — that's the official record.

8. Logging Waste Disposal

  1. Open your laboratory, then click the Waste Log tab.
  2. Click + Add Entry and describe what was disposed of, how, and by whom.
  3. Click Save Entry. No approval needed — it's recorded right away.

9. Reporting an Incident

  1. Open your laboratory, then click the Incident Report tab.
  2. Click + New Report and describe what happened.
  3. Submit it. It gets its own reference number automatically, and shows who filed it once signed.
  4. Print it, have those involved sign it by hand, then scroll down to Signed Hardcopy and click + Attach Signed Copy to upload a photo of the signed printout — that's the official record.

10. Printing a Form

  1. Open the form you want to print.
  2. Click the Print button near the top of the page.
  3. Your browser's print window will open. Portrait or landscape is already set for you — each form knows which way it's meant to print. Check that it looks correct, then print.
Tip: If you see an extra line of text (like a web address or date) at the top or bottom of the printed page that doesn't belong there, open "More settings" in the print window and turn off "Headers and footers". That extra line comes from the browser, not from the form.
Leave Margins on "Default" and Scale on 100%. The form works out where each page breaks based on those settings, so overriding them is what makes a page number come out wrong or a table run off the bottom. A4 and Letter both work without changing anything.

11. If You Get Stuck

  • Can't find a machine in a Schedule or Work Request dropdown? Those lists only show equipment units. Open the Inventory Sheet, make sure the item is tagged Equipment and saved, and its units will appear. A retired unit is deliberately left out.
  • Only one unit showing where you expect several? The number of 201 files follows the quantity on the Inventory Sheet. If the count says 1, correct the count and the missing units appear.
  • A request isn't moving? It's likely waiting for your Subject Coordinator or the office to act on it — that's normal, just check back later.
  • Still stuck? Ask your Subject Coordinator or your system admin for help.

Equipment Units & 201 Files

The Inventory Sheet F-LAB-010 is an aggregate count — one row, one quantity — while the Equipment Monitoring Record F-LAB-001 is a 201 file for one physical machine. Those two can't be the same record, so an item tagged Equipment is expanded into one unit per item in its balance.

  • Each unit carries its own serial number, location, service history and status, and a derived System Equipment ID of the form EQ-<item>-<unit> for internal reference. It isn't printed on the form — the serial number is what identifies the machine on paper.
  • Units are added automatically. Whenever the lab's equipment is loaded, the system tops the unit list up to match the stock balance. Tag an item Equipment with a balance of 5 and five 201 files exist from that moment; raise the balance to 7 and two more appear, already numbered unit 6 and unit 7.
  • Units are retired by hand. Retire posts a normal stock out-transaction of 1 alongside marking the unit Retired, so the balance drops with it and the auto-top-up doesn't immediately generate a replacement. Nothing is deleted: the 201 file and its history stay readable.
  • Serial numbers live only here. The Inventory Sheet no longer collects them — it can't, since one row stands for several machines.

How other forms use a unit

The Maintenance Schedule F-LAB-002, Calibration Schedule F-LAB-003 and Work Request F-LAB-004 all pick a unit rather than an item, and copy its serial number and location into the row at the moment it's picked. That copy is deliberate: a schedule that has already been printed and signed keeps showing what it was filed against even if the 201 file is corrected afterwards. Correct a live row by re-picking the unit.

Completing a Maintenance, Calibration or Work Request writes a Completed entry into that unit's 201 file automatically, carrying the request number across, so F-LAB-001 is a complete service history without anyone re-keying it.

How Printing Works

Clicking Print hides the sidebar and on-screen controls and opens the browser's print dialog for that form only.

  • The F-LAB header (school seal + "Page X of Y" label) repeats at the top of every physical page a form spans, and the form code and revision line (e.g. F-LAB-006 Rev. 0) prints in the footer of every page.
  • Each form declares its own orientation, so the print dialog opens already set correctly — nobody has to know that the wide log sheets are landscape.
  • Log-style forms pad in blank rows even when there are only a few real entries, so the printout fills a page the way the paper original does. Every printed row is floored at about 7 mm so a blank line is one somebody can actually write on.
  • "Page X of Y" is exact, not estimated. Browsers expose no page number to CSS or JavaScript, so a long form is split into one table per page ahead of time, each with its own header, and a page break is forced between them. How many rows go on a page is measured, not assumed: the table is laid out off-screen at the real printed width, every row's height is read, and pages are packed to what is genuinely left on the paper. A row whose equipment name wraps to four lines takes the space of four lines.
  • The split is measured against both A4 and US Letter and each row is charged the larger of its two shares of a page, so one printout is correct on either. The trade is a little unused space at the bottom of the sheet when the roomier paper is loaded.
  • Page margins are declared by the form. Leave the dialog's Margins on Default and Scale on 100% — the row measurement is done against those, and overriding them is what makes a table overflow and a page number repeat.

Request Approval Chain

Equipment Work Requests F-LAB-004 / F-LAB-005 and Other Requests (Bookstore / Supplies / BGU) share the same chain:

  1. Staff files a request — status starts as Pending.
  2. The lab's Subject Coordinator approves it, moving it to Filed.
  3. It appears in the cross-department inbox for any Secretary to pick up.
  4. Secretary sets it to In Progress, then Completed or Rejected.

Borrowing Requests F-LAB-007 are simpler: the Subject Coordinator approves directly, stamping a "Digitally signed by" line tied to their real account — no separate filing/secretary step.

Admin: Edit Item / Correct Balance

On the Stock Card page, admins get two extra actions next to Delete Item:

  • Edit Item — change name, category, unit of measure, reorder level, or notes.
  • Correct Balance — type the actual counted quantity from a physical/manual count; the system posts the signed difference as a normal transaction (same mechanism the Inventory Sheet's Apply uses), so the correction stays visible and auditable in the ledger rather than silently overwriting history.
Note: Equipment is intentionally excluded from Stock Cards, since that form is specifically the consumable quantity ledger. Equipment has its own record — F-LAB-001.

The category list is three values: Equipment, Tools & Materials and Consumables. Items still holding a value from the older six-option list are migrated on deploy (Materials and Glassware become Tools & Materials, Chemicals becomes Consumables); anything left as Other or blank shows as Uncategorized and needs re-tagging by hand, since neither maps to one new bucket confidently.

Troubleshooting / FAQ

My printout has the browser's own header/footer overlapping the form's.

Turn off "Headers and footers" in your browser's print dialog (under "More settings").

The equipment I need isn't in the schedule dropdown.

Those dropdowns list equipment units from F-LAB-001, not inventory items. Open the Inventory Sheet, tag the item as Equipment and save — its units appear at once. Retired units are excluded on purpose.

There are five of this machine but only one 201 file.

The number of units follows the item's stock balance, so a balance of 1 makes one unit. Correct the quantity on the Inventory Sheet (or the Stock Card's Correct Balance) and the missing units are created the next time the lab's equipment list loads.

Where do I type a serial number? The Inventory Sheet won't let me.

On F-LAB-001, per unit. One inventory row stands for several machines, so it has nowhere to put five different serial numbers. Open the Equipment Record tab and fill each unit in.

A machine was condemned. Do I delete it?

No — click Retire on its row. Deleting would take its service history with it, which is the part an audit asks for. Retiring keeps the 201 file, drops the inventory quantity by one, and stops a replacement unit being generated.

The same page number printed on two sheets.

Check the print dialog: Margins should be Default and Scale 100%. The form measures where its pages break against those settings, so a custom margin or a scale above 100% can push a table past the bottom edge, and the browser then splits it and repeats its header.

I don't see the Equipment Work Requests inbox.

It only shows for Secretary and Admin accounts, since it's the cross-department fulfillment queue.

A request I filed is stuck on "Filed."

It's waiting for a Secretary to pick it up and move it to In Progress — reach out if it's been sitting a while.
